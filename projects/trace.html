The problem behind the feature

Personal-data exposure detection asks an autonomous workflow to process potentially adversarial input. The system needs a way to control what can happen next, as well as a way to handle what it receives.

Trace puts those responsibilities into the pipeline structure through phase gates and sanitization.

What I designed

I designed a seven-phase autonomous detection pipeline with phase-gated execution and prompt-injection sanitization.

The pipeline was validated against eight simulated attack scenarios, including break-glass authorization bypass attempts.

A decision with consequences

I made phase progression an explicit part of the pipeline. A seven-phase structure gives execution boundaries a place in the architecture.

The tradeoff is between autonomous progression and controlled progression. Gates require explicit acceptance conditions; sanitization addresses the input side of the same problem.

Exercise the boundaries

The validation included eight simulated attack scenarios, including attempts to bypass break-glass authorization.

This connects the design to concrete adversarial situations: the execution boundary was something to exercise, as well as something to describe. The validation reported here is limited to those simulations.

Evidence and scope

The project record covers the seven-phase pipeline and eight simulated attack scenarios. Those simulations are the validation scope presented here.
